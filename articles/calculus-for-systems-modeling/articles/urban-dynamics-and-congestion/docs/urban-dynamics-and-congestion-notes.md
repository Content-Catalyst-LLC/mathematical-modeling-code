# Urban Dynamics and Congestion Notes

Urban dynamics and congestion models connect movement, density, flow, capacity, queues, travel time, route choice, induced demand, accessibility, land use, freight, curbside dynamics, environmental effects, and distributional burdens.

## Required distinctions

- vehicle movement versus person access
- flow versus stock accumulation
- traffic speed versus accessibility
- current congestion versus long-run induced demand
- user equilibrium versus system optimum
- local corridor effects versus network spillover
- average delay versus distributional burden
- scenario analysis versus forecast

## Review checklist

- Define network boundary, time scale, mode coverage, state variables, and units.
- Document demand, capacity, speed-density function, queueing rule, and travel-time function.
- Separate operating analysis from long-run land-use feedback.
- Preserve route choice, mode choice, induced demand, multimodal alternatives, environmental effects, equity outputs, uncertainty, sensitivity, validation scope, and claim boundaries.
