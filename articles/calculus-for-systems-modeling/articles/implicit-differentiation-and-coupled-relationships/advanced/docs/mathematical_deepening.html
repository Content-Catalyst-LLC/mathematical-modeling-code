Mathematical Deepening

Implicit differentiation is the differentiation of a constraint map. For \(F(x,y)=0\), allowed tangent directions satisfy \(F_x dx+F_y dy=0\). Local solvability for \(y\) requires \(F_y\neq 0\). In vector systems, the relevant Jacobian block must be invertible and sufficiently well conditioned.

Formal Implicit-Differentiation Conditions

Constraint Map

The relationship being held fixed must be stated explicitly.

Regularity

Local solvability requires a nonzero partial derivative or invertible Jacobian block.

Tangent Space

Allowed first-order movements lie in the kernel of the constraint derivative.

Branch Specificity

Implicit derivatives are local to a selected branch of the relationship.
