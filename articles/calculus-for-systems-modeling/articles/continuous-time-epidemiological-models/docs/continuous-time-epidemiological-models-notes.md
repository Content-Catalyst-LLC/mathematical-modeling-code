# Continuous-Time Epidemiological Models Notes

Continuous-time epidemiological models connect infection, recovery, exposure, immunity, vaccination, intervention, behavior, reporting, uncertainty, and claim boundaries.

## Required distinctions

- true infections versus reported cases
- incidence versus prevalence
- susceptible population versus total population
- infectiousness versus disease severity
- scenario versus public-health forecast
- educational model versus operational guidance
- \(R_0\) versus \(R_t\)
- homogeneous mixing versus structured contact
- transmission mechanism versus unexplained parameter change

## Review checklist

- Define population boundary, compartments, and units.
- Document transmission, recovery, latency, vaccination, waning, and reporting assumptions.
- Separate disease process from observation process.
- Preserve initial conditions, model purpose, data source, and calibration method.
- Include uncertainty, sensitivity, intervention mechanism, equity context, validation scope, and claim boundaries.
