# Infrastructure Flow and Capacity Dynamics Notes

Infrastructure flow and capacity models connect arrivals, service rates, throughput, utilization, queues, delays, bottlenecks, buffers, maintenance, decay, resilience, and claim boundaries.

## Required distinctions

- nominal capacity versus effective capacity
- average demand versus peak demand
- throughput versus service quality
- visible queue versus hidden backlog
- spare capacity versus waste
- storage buffer versus unlimited protection
- normal performance versus stress performance
- infrastructure scenario versus operational prediction

## Review checklist

- Define what flows, what stores, what constrains, and what counts as service.
- Document capacity definition, operating condition, and measurement boundary.
- Preserve arrival, service, utilization, queue, and delay records.
- Identify bottlenecks and downstream constraints.
- Document buffers, maintenance status, failure modes, and recovery assumptions.
- Attach uncertainty, sensitivity, equity impacts, governance context, validation scope, and claim boundaries.
