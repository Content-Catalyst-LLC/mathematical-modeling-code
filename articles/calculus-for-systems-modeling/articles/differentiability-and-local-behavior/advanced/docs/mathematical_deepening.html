Mathematical Deepening

This section strengthens the article for mathematically advanced readers by clarifying differentiability as a local approximation property rather than merely a symbolic derivative.

Formal Definitions

Derivative as First-Order Approximation

A function \(f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}\) is differentiable at \(a\) if \(f(a+h)=f(a)+f'(a)h+o(h)\).

Fréchet Derivative

For normed spaces \(X\) and \(Y\), Fréchet differentiability requires a bounded linear operator \(A:X\to Y\) whose approximation error is \(o(\|h\|)\).

Gâteaux Derivative

A Gâteaux derivative measures directional sensitivity. It may exist without a full Fréchet derivative.

Jacobian

For vector-valued models, the Jacobian is the local linear map when full differentiability holds.

Propositions and Counterexamples

Differentiability Implies Continuity

Any function differentiable at a point is continuous there. Discontinuity rules out differentiability.

Continuous but Not Differentiable

The function \(f(x)=|x|\) is continuous at \(0\) but not differentiable there.

Partial Derivatives Are Not Enough

A multivariable function may have partial derivatives at a point but fail to admit a coherent local linear approximation.

Modeling Warning

A numerical derivative can look stable on a coarse grid while missing a kink, threshold, or active-constraint change.
