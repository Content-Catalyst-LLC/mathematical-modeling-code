Mathematical Deepening

This section strengthens the article for mathematically advanced readers by clarifying formal definitions, preservation results, counterexamples, and modeling implications.

Formal Definitions

Topological Continuity

A function \(f:X\to Y\) is continuous if \(f^{-1}(U)\) is open in \(X\) whenever \(U\) is open in \(Y\).

Subspace Continuity

Continuity on a feasible domain is evaluated relative to the subspace topology. A model may be continuous on its valid domain without extending continuously outside it.

Uniform Continuity

Uniform continuity requires one input tolerance to work across the entire domain for each output tolerance.

Lipschitz Continuity

Lipschitz continuity gives quantitative control of output variation and supports stability, uniqueness, and error estimates.

Propositions and Counterexamples

Proposition: Differentiability Implies Continuity

If \(f\) is differentiable at \(a\), then \(f\) is continuous at \(a\). The converse is false.

Counterexample: Continuous but Not Differentiable

The function \(f(x)=|x|\) is continuous everywhere but not differentiable at \(0\).

Counterexample: Pointwise Limit

The functions \(f_n(x)=x^n\) on \([0,1]\) converge pointwise to a discontinuous limit.

Modeling Warning

A structural break may preserve continuity in level while changing slope, curvature, variance, or mechanism.
