# Ethics of Spatial Modeling

Spatial models can shape decisions about health services, policing, housing, climate risk, infrastructure, zoning, environmental regulation, and public investment.

## Risks

- boundaries group or divide communities misleadingly;
- risk maps stigmatize places;
- fine-grained location data compromises privacy;
- visibility bias overrepresents measured populations;
- resource allocation scores hide value judgments;
- maps imply precision beyond the underlying data;
- exploratory spatial outputs become authoritative decisions.

## Responsible practice

- document geometry and boundary choices;
- audit missingness and unequal data coverage;
- communicate positional, boundary, and interpolation uncertainty;
- avoid punitive overreach from risk maps;
- use aggregation and minimization for sensitive locations;
- make weighting and decision rules transparent.
